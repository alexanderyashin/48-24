# Costume, symbols and objects

[Русский](costume-symbols-and-objects-v0-1.md) · [Wiki](../../../../studio/navigation/en/README.md)

**Draft.** Normative specification for development. Status labels apply to individual source propositions; source consensus is not new canon approval. Translation awaits coauthor review.

[Source: SPOT 0.1, §16](../../../../world/world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-16) · Source status labels: SASHA'S FRAME; OPEN GAP; EDITORIAL PROPOSAL.

<a id="world_visual_costume"></a>

SASHA'S FRAME. Historical Russian imperial attributes provide a recognizable foundation. Specific uniforms, offices, decorations and clothing examples must relate to the selected baseline date and later reforms. They cannot be assembled from any period simply because individual details look imperial. Author-created alternative dress is nevertheless possible if the function and change that produced it are clear. [П1, П2, П7]

OPEN GAP. The state coat of arms, chancellorship symbols, secret-service emblems, district insignia and symbols of the future Union are not approved. Nor is a common system of ranks, insignia and service dress defined. A political sign needs an owner, audience and history of use. The same emblem on a church, a commodity and a military vehicle cannot be treated as random ornament if the audience is expected to read it as part of the world.

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. For a key object, it is sufficient to define material, manufacturing method, user, signs of use and repair location. An inscription belongs to its surface: it follows the form, ageing and application method. A decorative element expresses the object’s origin or status. This rule supports physical credibility and allows the object to appear in different shots without visual contradiction.
