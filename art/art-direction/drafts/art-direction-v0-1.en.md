# Initial art direction for 48/24

[Русский](art-direction-v0-1.md) · [Wiki](../../../studio/navigation/en/README.md)

**Draft.** Normative specification for development. Status labels apply to individual source propositions; source consensus is not new canon approval. Translation awaits coauthor review.

[Source: SPOT 0.1](../../../world/world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md) · Source status labels: CONSENSUS; SASHA'S FRAME; IVAN'S PROPOSAL; EDITORIAL PROPOSAL; OPEN GAP.

A working brief for the first visual experiments. It combines the initial propositions of [SPOT 0.1, section 16](../../../world/world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-16) with Sasha’s clarification concerning pre-revolutionary typography, architecture, costume, hostile weather and contrasting interiors. That clarification is Sasha’s requirement from the project context of 19 November 2026; Ivan’s agreement is not implied. New execution methods below are marked as editorial proposals.

## Foundation

**Consensus recorded in SPOT:** coal and steam shape the industrial environment; electricity exists and access is uneven; social contrast is legible through clothing, light, cleanliness and living conditions. Stolypin is mechanised gradually and retains human traits at the beginning of the main story. These are conceptual foundations, not approved character images. [CAN004–CAN009]

**Sasha’s frame:** recognizable pre-revolutionary architecture, costume and typography. Steampunk should be heavy, dark and connected to functioning technology. Outside are hostile cold, wind and slanting rain mixed with snow. Affluent interiors provide warmth, steam heating, electric light and rich materials; institutions have a more austere Victorian environment and gas lighting; poor districts have coal, firewood, drafts and insufficient heat. [PROJECT-BRIEF-2026-11-19]

**Ivan’s proposal:** “steam-powered Gothic noir in a Russian folkloric idiom”. This is a direction for discussing expressiveness, not an assertion of literal magic or a set of folkloric creatures. [SPOT, §16; CAN051 retains its earlier-version status]

## Three environments for initial experiments

| Environment | What should be legible | Materials and light sources | What is not yet approved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wealthy residential interior | Protection from cold, servicing and prosperity | Steam heating; electric fixtures; maintained wood, fabric, glass and metal | Specific woods, fixtures, coats of arms and the architectural style of each building |
| State institution | Distance, order and limited comfort | Gas light; heavy working surfaces; institutional furniture; signs of continuous use | Agency names, offices, service symbols and uniforms |
| Poor district or workers’ housing | The cost of labour and shortage of warmth | Coal and firewood; temporary repairs; wear; damp and traces of fuel | Poverty as a moral judgment on a character, or identical appearance across all districts |

The shared outside world should connect these spaces through weather, logistics and objects. The exact technical profiles of heating, gas and electrical supply are checked together with worldbuilding. Electric light need not be blue: colour and contrast follow staging rather than an arbitrary “technology = colour” code.

## Working with objects

**Editorial proposals based on SPOT:**

- Every pipe, lever, valve, boiler and wire has a function, scale, connection and means of servicing.
- Materials retain physical behaviour: wet things look wet, metal and cloth reflect light differently, and light sources explain shadows.
- Wear occurs at points of contact, load, repair and weather exposure. Random uniform dirt does not substitute for an object’s history.
- An inscription belongs to its surface, following its perspective, curvature, application method and overall wear.
- A symbol has an owner and purpose. Until coats of arms and emblems are chosen, retain them as open questions in the specification.
- A character’s recognizability is checked across shots and biographical stages. One striking image does not establish a canonical appearance.

## Palette and typography

No HEX palettes or typefaces are approved yet. Experiments record the colour’s purpose, lighting conditions, environment and image version. Black coal, a cold wet street and a warm interior are conceptual roles that still need translation into specific values.

For typefaces, check rights to use and redistribute the file, Cyrillic support, required pre-reform characters, Latin characters and numerals. A requirement for pre-revolutionary character does not establish mandatory old orthography in every interface and publication. An image of a historical sign and the licence of a modern font file are different sources of rights.

Typeface, palette and symbol candidates remain in the relevant area’s `drafts/`. A font file is not added without a clear basis for redistribution; a commercial source file or someone else’s artwork does not become our asset by being placed in a folder.

## An independent visual language

LOGION and other projects do not automatically become the visual canon of 48/24. Other works are used as research references for specific techniques, with their sources identified. Ready-made characters, emblems, costumes and recognizable compositions belonging to others are not transferred. Historical brands and the Shustov motif require separate source research and checks on permissible use of specific materials.

## What to record with an experiment

The shot’s purpose; author and responsible person; linked world records; date and version; the character’s biographical stage; location and weather; light source; references used and rights basis; tool, model and available generation settings; human changes; known visual errors; and the author’s decision on the result. Unknown values receive an explicit label rather than invented precision.

A selected image may become a reference for colour, material or composition within a stated scope. It does not automatically approve story details, coats of arms or devices that AI happened to add to the shot.

## Open decisions

- Overall degree of realism and painterliness; a set of stable visual references.
- Historical foundations for costumes, objects and architecture by specific period.
- Palettes, typefaces, coats of arms, emblems, rank insignia and a system of inscriptions.
- Appearance and stages of the ruler’s mechanisation; the fate of the mechanical Alexei image.
- Sound language, music, speech patterns and regional differences.

Related questions: GAP015–GAP023, GAP035–GAP037, GAP045–GAP047, GAP055, GAP058–GAP059; divergences DIV005–DIV007, DIV019–DIV022.
