# Reading the SPOT and statuses of source positions

[Русский](spot-reading-and-statuses-v0-1.md) · [Wiki](../../navigation/en/README.md)

**Draft.** A normative draft for collaborative development. Authorial status applies to the exact source statement; migration and translation do not create new coauthor agreement. Translation awaits human review.

[Source: SPOT 0.1, §1](../../../world/world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-1) · [Current canon policy](../../CANON_POLICY.md).

Source statuses: EDITORIAL PROPOSAL.

**Applying the source text.** References below to Word, “this document,” and the title Kiberstolypin describe the original SPOT edition. The current working name is 48/24; the two wikis provide current navigation. Current repository rules take precedence over editorial procedures in the source.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-0 -->

This document provides a single record of the Kiberstolypin project for Alexander Yashin and Ivan Konyushkov. It brings together the epic's premise, the structure of its world, story and character arcs, artistic reference points, and decisions needed for further coauthorship. Working version 0.1, dated 19 November 2026, records the current state of development.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-1 -->

The document is organized by subject. Each section distinguishes agreed positions, authorial frames, proposals, incompatible alternatives, and unknown elements. Completeness means covering the significant areas of development, not declaring every area finished. An open question remains part of the overall picture until a decision is made.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-2 -->

SPOT stands for Single Point of Truth: a unified record of the project's state. The CAN register contains the wording and statuses of its principal positions. Thematic chapters explain their meaning and consequences. DIV cards describe divergences, GAP cards identify missing definitions, CHAR cards describe characters, and ARC cards cover storylines. These labels are working identifiers in the document, not names within the fictional world.

## How to read the document

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-4 -->

For an initial introduction, read the concept of the epic, the rules of the world, and the historical cycles. When writing a scene, also check the relevant character, location, technology, and storyline. The divergence register and priority decisions support the consolidation of authorial positions. Geography, material culture, historical foundations, and the gap register support research.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-5 -->

The table of contents contains active links and is implemented as a Word field. After editing the text, update the entire field using Update Field. Headings are available in the navigation pane. Identifiers in indexes and textual references lead to the relevant cards wherever such cards are defined. Each major section has a link back to the table of contents.

| Coauthor's task | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| Find out what has already been accepted | The canon-position register and the status of the specific CAN entry |
| Choose between disputed alternatives | The divergence register and the corresponding DIV card |

## Statuses of positions

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-8 -->

A status applies to a specific statement. Agreement with the general direction does not imply acceptance of every detail in the surrounding text. The absence of an objection does not replace an explicit decision. Sasha's authorial frame remains a requirement of the project's initiator; it is not relabeled as agreement by both coauthors.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-9 -->

| Status | Meaning and use |
| --- | --- |
| CONSENSUS | The materials show an explicit convergence of positions or direct acceptance. Within the stated wording, this is an agreed working foundation. |
| SASHA'S FRAME | A direct requirement or sustained direction from Alexander Yashin. Ivan's acceptance does not follow automatically. |
| JOINTLY DEVELOPED | Both authors use and expand the concept; its precise parameters have not yet been approved. |
| SASHA'S PROPOSAL | A specific option proposed by Alexander Yashin that has not become a joint decision. |
| IVAN'S PROPOSAL | A specific option proposed by Ivan Konyushkov that has not become a joint decision. |
| DIVERGENCE | Different alternatives are present in the work. They must not be silently combined. |
| OPEN GAP | A necessary area has not yet been defined sufficiently. This is not permission to fill it with arbitrary details. |
| EDITORIAL PROPOSAL | A connection, formulation, or working method suggested during systematization. An authorial decision is required before it can enter canon. |
| EARLIER VERSION | A previously proposed option that is not automatically carried into the latest construction. Its useful elements may be considered separately. |
| CANCELLED | The option was explicitly rejected. It does not return to the current version without a new decision. |

## Status and historical accuracy

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-11 -->

Agreement between the authors and the accuracy of a historical claim are separate attributes. A fictional event can be firmly canonical. A real fact may be reliably supported by a source without yet being used in the world. A historical hypothesis or legend does not become a fact merely because it is useful to the plot.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-12 -->

The document distinguishes four layers: the historical baseline before the divergence; fictional events in the alternate history; inferences about possible consequences of those events; and editorial recommendations. ИСТ labels identify external sources. П labels identify the original authorial materials. Р1 identifies an editorial analysis, which is not itself a decision by the coauthors.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-13 -->

An accusation made by a character, state propaganda, or an in-world rumor is not equivalent to the authors' account of reality. For example, accusations that Rasputin or aristocrats are connected to Britain can exist in the story regardless of whether the authors confirm those connections as real. Such cases require separate records of the actual event, the public account, and what the characters know.

## Scope of the current record

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-15 -->

The main historical anchors are the divergence in 1911 and the beginning of the main narrative in 1941. Characters, events, institutional names, borders, and routes that have not yet been defined retain the relevant markers. Colloquial labels such as CIA, FSB, and GRU express the functions of the services; they are not accepted names of organizations in the world.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-16 -->

Kiberstolypin is used as the project's working title. The names of the future universe, its individual cycles, and the public series remain subject to a separate decision. Rzhevsky, the Steam Sovereign, the Copper Tsar, and the Mechanical Chancellor retain the roles and versions specified in their cards; they are not automatically treated as names for the same character.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-17 -->

The document includes information about the world's future and the eventual directions of its arcs. It is intended for coauthorship. A public synopsis and audience presentation should be derived separately, with their own limits on revealing events.

## Dependencies to check when making changes

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-19 -->

Status of this procedure: EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. Checking dependencies helps assess the consequences of changes before they are propagated into dependent materials.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-20 -->

If the historical divergence changes, review the international system, biographies, enterprises, and technologies. If the nature of mechanization changes, review the ruler's political agency, the capabilities available to other people, and the final transition between eras. If the economic structure of the districts changes, review the origins of the revolution and the future Union.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-1 block-21 -->

Changing one element does not require rewriting all adjacent chapters in advance. It requires identifying the affected dependencies and determining which of them actually change. An unresolved dependency remains visible until it is checked.
