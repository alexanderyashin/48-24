# SPOT proposals for canon governance

[Русский](spot-governance-proposals-v0-1.md) · [Wiki](../../navigation/en/README.md)

**Draft.** A normative draft for collaborative development. Authorial status applies to the exact source statement; migration and translation do not create new coauthor agreement. Translation awaits human review.

[Source: SPOT 0.1, §22](../../../world/world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-22) · [Current canon policy](../../CANON_POLICY.md).

Source statuses: EDITORIAL PROPOSAL.

**Scope limit.** The SPOT editorial proposals are preserved below. They do not change the [workflow](../../WORKFLOW.md), [metadata rules](../../NAMING_AND_METADATA.md), or the procedure for genuine authorial approval.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-0 -->

<a id="gov_object"></a>

## The object of a shared decision

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-1 -->

Status of this section's rules: EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. The unit of collaborative work is a position, character, state of a location, technology rule, or event. A change must specify precisely the wording being accepted and the earlier statements affected.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-2 -->

In the materials, Sasha mainly focuses on the mechanics of the universe, and Ivan on plots and human relationships. This is a distribution of interests. The right to change canon unilaterally, approve characters, or release materials requires a separate agreement.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-3 -->

<a id="gov_change"></a>

## Proposed change procedure

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-4 -->

The author identifies the affected identifiers, the new wording, its reason, and its consequences. An alternative receives its own designation and is preserved alongside the current version. Alternatives are combined only after a substantive decision by the coauthors.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-5 -->

| Step | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Describe the change | One clear formulation and a list of affected positions. |
| Check compatibility | Consequences for chronology, characters, the world, and existing materials are identified. |
| Coauthor decision | The selected option, the limits of agreement, and the remaining questions are recorded. |
| Update the SPOT | The corresponding cards, links, and status are updated. |
| Review works | Scenes and visual materials requiring correction are identified. |

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-6 -->

Where there is no agreement, DIVERGENCE status remains. Independent work can continue. A dependent work may be developed in a clearly labeled experimental branch, without automatically transferring its events into shared canon.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-7 -->

<a id="gov_ids"></a>

## Identifiers and versions

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-8 -->

Identifiers are retained when wording is refined. When objects are split or combined, the earlier cards link to the new ones. Renaming preserves the connection to existing scenes.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-9 -->

A version is a development snapshot containing positions with different statuses. Version 0.1 records the initial state; further version numbers and release rules are determined by the coauthors. The change log records new wording, its reason, and affected materials.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-10 -->

Conflicts between revisions are resolved according to the authorial decision and its scope. A later file date or an accidental contradiction in a new scene does not imply a revision of canon.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-11 -->

<a id="gov_causal"></a>

## Checking causality and continuity

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-12 -->

| Area to check | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Chronology | Do people, institutions, and technologies have enough time to undergo the necessary development? |
| Knowledge | Can the character know precisely what they rely on when acting? |
| Resources | Which people, materials, money, and routes make the action possible? |
| Interests | Why does the participant make the decision, and what do they risk? |
| Rules | Does the technology or institution work consistently in comparable circumstances? |
| Consequences | What will change after the event, and where will that change be visible? |
| The state of the world | Have earlier constraints, allies, or dependencies disappeared without explanation? |
| Authorial status | Has a proposal been presented as an agreed position? |

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-13 -->

This check supports the world's consistency. The audience needs only the necessary observable consequence; detailed dependencies remain in the authors' cards.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-14 -->

<a id="gov_knowledge"></a>

## A character's facts and the world's facts

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-15 -->

For intrigue, distinguish the actual event, the account being circulated, the character's knowledge, and the audience's information. This is particularly important for the Kyiv conspiracy, the Romanovs' fate, Britain's role, and the ruler's mechanization.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-16 -->

A subjective memory or official reconstruction is marked in the script card. Without such a marker, a flashback is perceived as confirmation of the objective past.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-17 -->

<a id="gov_done"></a>

## Criteria for a world version's readiness

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-18 -->

The world is ready for a specific work when the necessary rules and premises are defined, direct incompatibilities are removed, and the consequences of events are understood. There is no need to develop every country and century in advance.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-22 block-19 -->

For the 1941 version, define the source of authority, Stolypin's condition, ownership, technologies, supply, the protagonist's position, and the initial contradictions. For the revolutionary cycle, define the reasons allies change sides, the stages of struggle, and the new order. For the ending Sasha proposes, define the Union's contradictions and the conditions of its breakup.
