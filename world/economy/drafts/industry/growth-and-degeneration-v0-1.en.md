# Growth and subsequent degeneration

[Русский](growth-and-degeneration-v0-1.md) · [Wiki](../../../../studio/navigation/en/README.md)

**Draft.** Normative specification for development. Status labels apply to individual source propositions; source consensus is not new canon approval. Translation awaits coauthor review.

[Source: SPOT 0.1, §7](../../../world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-7) · Source status labels: OPEN GAP; EDITORIAL PROPOSAL.

<a id="world_economy_growth"></a>

OPEN GAP. The authors explicitly ask why the chosen arrangement first delivers rapid industrial growth and subsequently obstructs further development. No answer has yet been recorded. A general promise of inevitable revolution cannot substitute for one: the specific economy needs a sequence of benefits, dependencies and accumulated constraints. [П7]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. One possible mechanism is that a large protected district initially assembles capital, personnel and infrastructure where isolated participants could not do so quickly. The state gains a reliable supplier and a governable territory. Later, the same guarantees entrench the owner, restrict competitors’ access and make the movement of people, knowledge and resources conditional on permission. The system keeps reproducing a successful initial solution even after the task changes. [Р1]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. A wartime crisis may intensify the contradiction: the centre demands greater output and common standards, owners protect revenue, specialists demand change and workers bear the costs. The repressive apparatus treats failures as disloyalty, worsening the shortage of reliable information. This is one possible connection between economic and political degeneration. It does not assert that private property, centralisation or any other organisational form must follow the same path.

To adopt this model, the authors must establish which early successes actually occurred, which groups benefited and why familiar corrective mechanisms stopped working. Without these distinctions, the revolutionary coalition remains a collection of people united only by dissatisfaction with one ruler. The new programme under discussion should answer identifiable economic problems rather than merely promise a benevolent manager.
