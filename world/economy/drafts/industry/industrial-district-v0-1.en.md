# The industrial district as a worldbuilding unit

[Русский](industrial-district-v0-1.md) · [Wiki](../../../../studio/navigation/en/README.md)

**Draft.** Normative specification for development. Status labels apply to individual source propositions; source consensus is not new canon approval. Translation awaits coauthor review.

[Source: SPOT 0.1, §7](../../../world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-7) · Source status labels: SASHA'S PROPOSAL; EDITORIAL PROPOSAL.

<a id="world_economy_district"></a>

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. A district can usefully be described as an interconnected economic territory: core production, sources of raw materials and energy, workers’ settlements, skilled services, an outward transport route and an external market. Some functions may belong to the enterprise owner, others to the state or independent municipal institutions. This is a working model for development, not established uniformity across the empire. [Р1]

Such a district creates a coherent connection between high politics and everyday life. A tariff change affects enterprise revenue; an order reduction affects employment; a fuel conflict affects heating and output; a route closure changes food prices. A character can experience a social crisis through a specific loss without lecturing on the world’s economic structure. Each connection needs a decision-maker and someone capable of resisting that decision.

SASHA'S PROPOSAL. While still in state service, the protagonist might investigate an industrialists’ conspiracy or suppress a district uprising. This allows him to meet future opponents and allies before his revolutionary turn. The mission itself is not approved. Its targets are undefined: owners, engineers, workers, local administration or a temporary alliance of several forces. [П7]

| Economic participant | Interest | Development question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| District owner | Income, control of property and preservation of privileges | What the owner receives from the centre and can retain independently |
| Engineers and foremen | Professional working conditions and standing | How they acquire knowledge, move between places and protect their labour |
| Wage workers | Pay, housing, safety and the ability to leave | What binds the worker to the factory apart from wages |
| Unfree workers | Survival and a change in their situation | Legal basis, numbers and role in production |
| Trade intermediaries | Access to goods, routes and settlement of accounts | Where permitted trade ends and grey-market trade begins |
| State purchasers | Reliable fulfilment and political loyalty | How price, quality, deadlines and the apparatus’s interests relate |
