# Labour, skills and the reproduction of society

[Русский](labour-skills-and-social-reproduction-v0-1.md) · [Wiki](../../../../studio/navigation/en/README.md)

**Draft.** Normative specification for development. Status labels apply to individual source propositions; source consensus is not new canon approval. Translation awaits coauthor review.

[Source: SPOT 0.1, §7](../../../world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-7) · Source status labels: JOINTLY DEVELOPED; OPEN GAP; EDITORIAL PROPOSAL.

<a id="world_economy_labor"></a>

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. Discussing penal labour immediately exposes a separate problem of engineering personnel. Large-scale production requires not only people for heavy work but a sustainable reproduction of knowledge. Prisoners therefore cannot fill every staffing need without an explanation of specialisms, training, transmission of experience and motivation. The correspondence raises this question but does not resolve it. [П7]

OPEN GAP. Schools, technical colleges, higher education, factory apprenticeships, military specialist training and recruitment of foreigners are undefined. It is not established whether a talented worker can become an engineer, who pays for education or whether graduates owe obligations to an enterprise. These answers directly affect the protagonist’s possible biography and the future conflict between the old elite and a new professional stratum.

OPEN GAP. The districts’ social infrastructure is not yet described: housing, food supply, hospitals, water, heating, transport, childcare and assistance for workplace injuries. Ownership by the state, factory proprietor, community or workers’ association creates different dependencies. Even a well-run enterprise may restrict a person’s freedom if losing a job also means losing every available provision for daily life. This is a possible fictional situation, not the established arrangement of all districts.

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. A minimal social profile requires three biographies for each depicted district: a worker with a family, a specialist with freedom of choice, and a person without the freedom to leave. Comparing their access to housing, education, treatment and justice will show the structure of society more precisely than a general assertion that “everyone lives badly”. Differences between districts will provide real motives for migration, escape, recruitment and labour conflicts.
