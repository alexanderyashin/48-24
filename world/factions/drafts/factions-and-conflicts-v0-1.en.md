## Social forces and political organisations

SASHA'S FRAME. The principal characters are connected to social forces and express their position within the historical process. Examples include property owners, industrialists, peasants, workers, managers, and various ideological positions. This is a list of functions to develop, not an approved party system. The existence of a social interest does not demonstrate the existence of an organisation, a shared leader, a single programme, or discipline. [П1]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. A faction should be defined through its participants, resources, demands, decision-making method, and limits of acceptable compromise. Nationality, religion, and social estate may affect a person's position but do not determine an unchanging character. A British or Russian agent's state affiliation describes their service, not the moral essence of a people. Each individual should remain capable of mistakes, negotiation, and different behaviour in different circumstances. [Р1]

| Social force | Foundation | Subject of conflict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Chancellor's apparatus | Orders and an administrative network | Continuity of power and the limits of control |
| Dynastic supporters | Succession and symbolic legitimacy | The right to govern in the name of the Crown |
| Industrial owners | Enterprises and access to resources | Autonomy and distribution of income |
| Worker collectives | Labour and the continued operation of production | Living conditions and participation in decisions |
| Engineering community | Knowledge and maintenance of machinery | Professional freedom and dependence on the client |
| Church circles | Religious authority and institution | Legitimacy and the limits of cooperation |
| Free communities | Local organisation and armed force | Control of territory and routes |
| Smuggling networks | Connections and illicit exchange | Access to markets and protection from prosecution |

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. The table organises forces already named and identifies questions for their development. It does not establish a specific programme shared by all workers, all engineers, or the entire Church. The demands of individual organisations will need clarification once the economic model and local conditions have been chosen. [Р1]

## Initial conflicts and later clarifications

EARLIER VERSION. At the beginning of the discussion, monarchists were associated with practical magic and the left with technological means. Ivan proposed that magic and technology would displace one another. The next portion states that magic has been abandoned, and the explanation of the world shifts first to infection, then to Stolypin's alternative history. The original scheme of ‘magical right against technological left’ must therefore not quietly return to the working faction system. [П1; П2]

SASHA'S FRAME. Later, expensive electrical infrastructure is explicitly associated with wealthy conservative groups, while reformers may use simple accessible means. Political position and technical equipment therefore do not automatically coincide. Membership of a new movement should be determined by attitudes towards the organisation of society, not by weapon type or energy source. [П4]

## Main axes of confrontation

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. Within the state apparatus, preserving governability conflicts with an expanding repressive function. During the regime's formation, special structures help the Chancellor bypass old obstacles. During its decline, the same concentration of power can turn against former allies. Personnel's move into opposition is proposed to arise from changes in their conditions of service and threats from their own state. [П4–П6]

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. Material interdependence develops between the capital and manufacturing districts. The capital needs coal, metal, machines, and skilled labour; the districts depend on arrangements for exchange, transport, and political decisions. Sasha proposes looking for revolutionary potential in this structure. OPEN GAP: exactly what conflict arises—between owners and the state, workers and owners, different industrial groups, or simultaneously at several levels? [П7]

DIVERGENCE. Dynastic opposition, industrial discontent, and the revolutionary movement have not yet been separated into independent programmes. An enemy of Stolypin is not necessarily a supporter of the future Union. Likewise, a participant in illicit trade may exploit the crisis without wanting regime change. It must be determined who can cooperate temporarily, what price they demand for an alliance, and why the coalition breaks up or survives after victory. [П3; П6; П7]

## The revolutionary coalition

SASHA'S FRAME. A mass shift of familiar characters from the imperial camp into opposition requires a substantial shared basis. Repression, the system consuming itself, and the impossibility of continuing their former work have been proposed. The protagonist should connect forces already emerging and turn discontent into a constructive programme. This preserves continuity of people and skills between regimes. [П6]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. Three things should be recorded for every future coalition member: what they lose under the old order, what they demand from the new one, and what they risk by joining. This avoids explaining every change of allegiance through personal affection for the protagonist. Compatibility of demands matters more than simultaneous hatred of the Chancellor. [Р1]

## Factions of the next era

SASHA'S PROPOSAL. After the revolution, production associations of workers resembling guilds or corporations with their own territories and enclaves are envisaged. OPEN GAP: who becomes a member of such a corporation, how the rights of people outside major industries are secured, and who controls intercorporate exchange, armed forces, and collective decisions. The names of imperial factions cannot yet be carried into the next era without checking how their interests change. [П1; П7]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. The second cycle should show new relationships and new achievements; otherwise it becomes a mechanical repetition of the first. The protagonist's late conflict arises from the specific development of the society he created. His opponents need not repeat the roles of the secret police or Rasputin. The next era requires its own matrix of forces, which is still absent from the supplied correspondence. [Р1]
