## How to read the timeline

CONSENSUS. The correspondence explicitly agrees two anchor dates: the Kyiv assassination attempt in 1911 and the beginning of the main narrative in 1941. Sasha proposes showing the preceding thirty years mainly through flashbacks; Ivan accepts the initial temporal framework. This does not mean that every intervening event and every war has already been defined. Within the timeline, a historical date, an event in the alternative history, and an approximate calculation of an arc's duration must be distinguished. [П4]

SASHA'S FRAME. The real historical baseline applies before the point of divergence. Geography and inherited conditions remain after it, but political events, the fortunes of enterprises, and technologies must be connected anew through causes and consequences. A later message counts as a clarification only where it actually changes an earlier decision. An idea's absence from later discussion is not equivalent to its explicit cancellation. [П2; П7]

## First cycle: Stolypin's rise

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. Surviving the assassination attempt changes Stolypin's convictions. He discovers a threat at the top, loses faith in Nicholas's ability to hold the state together, and begins independently restructuring the system of power. Sasha establishes the shift from serving the monarch to governing the empire himself. Ivan develops it into a sequence of emergency rule, special committees, a parliamentary act, isolation of the court, and the creation of new legitimacy. The detailed mechanisms of this sequence remain Ivan's proposal. [П2–П4]

DIVERGENCE. The political arrangement varies between a constitutional monarchy, the formal retention of the tsar under the Chancellor's effective rule, and the Chancellor's power following the dynasty's physical elimination. Another scheme presented Alexei as the official tsar with Stolypin as the power behind the throne. These constructions cannot simultaneously be treated as one legal system. The monarch's powers, the basis of the chancellorship, the fate of succession, and the existence of a functioning monarchy in 1941 must be chosen. [П2–П4]

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. Rasputin is to participate in the regime's formation. The discussion includes his independent struggle against Stolypin, his use through blackmail, discrediting the court, and accusations of a connection to England. These events belong to fictional alternative history; the coauthors' remarks about their historical plausibility do not turn the proposed intrigue into an established historical fact. The date of its resolution and its connection to the elimination of the Romanovs are undefined. [П6]

<a id="nar0203"></a>

## Stability and accumulating contradictions

SASHA'S FRAME. A period of convincing success for Stolypin must exist between his rise and decline. Otherwise the protagonist's loyalty, the regime's longevity, and the durability of its social foundations are unclear. Sasha distinguishes three stages: rise, rule with visible internal contradictions, and collapse. Ivan proposes sustaining legitimacy through withdrawal from war, agrarian reforms, railway construction, and a cult of the effective ruler. The concrete achievements, beneficiaries, and hidden costs of this period require development. [П4; П5]

OPEN GAP. The sequence of technological development between 1911 and 1941 is undefined. There is a general principle of inertia in steam infrastructure and expensive electricity, but no dates for the emergence of key civilian and military machines, the stages of Stolypin's mechanisation, the development of manufacturing districts, or the emergence of their political autonomy. The thirty years should explain the state of the world rather than serve as an empty interval. [П4; П7]

<a id="nar0204"></a>

## Wars and international periods

DIVERGENCE. The initial macrohistory described a protracted war between Russia and the British Empire, Russia's defeat, a revolutionary Union, and its victory over Britain. A later version introduces a 1941 campaign against a united German and Austro-Hungarian power, European consolidation, and renewed British dominance. The degree of German unification and France's position also change. These are different geopolitical schemes, not successive stages that have already been agreed. [П1; П2]

OPEN GAP. The dates of Russia's withdrawal from the First World War, the duration of the remaining war, treaties, borders, and the conditions for entering a new war are undefined. There are references to Second and Third Imperialist Wars and a proposed intervening period of building a new society. Their names, participants, and correspondence to historical wars are unestablished. The document must not automatically treat every reference to ‘WWII’ as the historical Second World War with its known sides and dates. [П1; П2; П4]

## Working duration of the revolutionary transition

EARLIER VERSION. The empire's defeat around 1945 and Stolypin's overthrow around 1948 were initial approximate dates. They precede discussion of a longer transition and should not automatically be used as the current calendar. [П4]

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. The authors subsequently discuss ten to fifteen years of crisis and transition after 1941, relating that duration to the protagonist's maturation. The precise boundaries between war, rising repression, and the revolutionary phase have not yet been separated. [П5]

SASHA'S PROPOSAL. The latest extended estimate separates about ten years of deterioration from a subsequent five to seven years of underground struggle. This is a working estimate within a jointly developed chronology. Neither a fifteen-to-seventeen-year duration nor a revolutionary calendar has received final approval. [П6]

| Stage | Later estimate | What must be established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accumulating crisis | About ten years after 1941 | War and internal turning points |
| The protagonist's break with the regime | At the boundary between the two stages | The mentor's fate and personal risk |
| Underground struggle | A further five to seven years | Sequence of failures and formation of a programme |
| Revolutionary victory | Provisionally 1956–1958 | The date is conditional and has not been fixed |
| Building the new society | Decades | Wars and institutional stages |

SASHA'S PROPOSAL. The path to victory includes a personal attempt to eliminate Stolypin, unsuccessful cooperation with disaffected people and the exposure of a conspiracy, followed by the creation of a small motivated group with a positive programme for a new order. Ivan proposes linking another assassination attempt to the Chancellor's further mechanisation; Sasha allows that the protagonist's attack may be what finally binds him to the machine. This is a causal outline, not an approved sequence of completed episodes. [П6]

## Age and lifespan

Historical anchor: Pyotr Stolypin was born in 1862. He is forty-nine in September 1911; during 1941 he is seventy-eight or seventy-nine, depending on the scene's date. If the revolutionary resolution falls in 1956–1958, he reaches approximately ninety-four to ninety-six after the relevant birthday. Mechanisation must therefore have defined limits for extending life. Calculating age establishes neither how long personhood is retained nor a permissible biological mechanism. [ИСТ01; П4–П6]

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. The central intelligence officer is proposed to be around twenty-five in 1941. Ten years later he is around thirty-five; after the full fifteen-to-seventeen-year transition, around forty to forty-two. A further thirty years of activity would put him at around seventy to seventy-two in the late part of the cycle. His precise birth year, time of death, and need for life extension are undefined. Ages should be recalculated after every change to the overall chronology. [П4–П6]

<a id="nar0207"></a>

## The second cycle and the epic's boundary

SASHA'S FRAME. The epic contains two historical cycles: the creation and exhaustion of Stolypin's order, followed by the creation and exhaustion of the central protagonist's order. The protagonist's death sets the final boundary of his biography and of the work. This framework does not automatically define the state form of the next era or the specific mechanism of its ending. [П4; П6]

SASHA'S PROPOSAL. The specific form of the new society is a Union of Socialist Corporations, in which workers' membership of production associations matters more than territorial-national divisions. An arc of external victory, expansion of the system, and subsequent disintegration is also proposed. Other formulations use the terms Republic and socialism. A single name, the legal structure, and this particular geopolitical sequence require agreement. [П1; П4; П7]

OPEN GAP. The scope of the proposed final victory varies between the entire continent and the entire world. The new order's duration, its internal coalitions, the protagonist's role in governing it, the cause of its loss of viability, and the form of its disintegration are unestablished. SASHA'S PROPOSAL: the ending may hint at a subsequent, transhumanist era. This hint does not yet describe an established fourth civilisation or require its portrayal. [П4; П6]
