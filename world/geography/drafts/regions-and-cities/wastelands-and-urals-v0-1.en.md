# The Wastelands and the mining and metallurgical Urals

[Source: SPOT 0.1, §8](../../../world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-8) · Source status labels: JOINTLY DEVELOPED; IVAN'S PROPOSAL; EDITORIAL PROPOSAL.

<a id="world_geography_ural"></a>

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. The “Wastelands” gradually change from a general label for territory beyond the Urals into a space of industrial districts, camps, free settlements, smuggling routes and local authorities. This is an inhabited economic periphery vital to the centre. A sandy desert is not a required landscape. The scope of the name, its official or colloquial status and its exact boundaries remain undefined. [П3, П4, П7]

IVAN'S PROPOSAL. The image of enclaves combines a factory settlement, deforested surroundings and weakly developed space between districts. Nizhny Tagil, Yekaterinburg, Nevyansk, Polevskoy and historical Ural production sites are named as important real anchors. Ivan’s personal experience provides visual and spatial observations; it does not replace checking the dates of enterprises, routes and the selected industrial technology. [П7]

HISTORICAL BASIS. Research into fuels used by Ural metallurgy in 1882–1911 shows different plant profiles: charcoal, firewood, local mineral coal, petroleum fuels and wood substitutes were used differently according to production and location. The Urals cannot be described as a homogeneous store of interchangeable fuels and ores. [ИСТ05]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. Several levels of periphery are worth distinguishing: a functioning district, a settlement along a route, an area of seasonal economic activity and an inaccessible territory. Their inhabitants do not become one social group because of the shared name “Wastelands”. Differences motivate cooperation and conflict among industrialists, engineers, migrants, local communities, Cossacks and representatives of the centre.
