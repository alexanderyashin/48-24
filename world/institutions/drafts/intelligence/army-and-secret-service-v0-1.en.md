# The army and the secret service

[Русский](army-and-secret-service-v0-1.md) · [Wiki](../../../../studio/navigation/en/README.md)

**Draft.** Normative specification for development. Status labels apply to individual source propositions; source consensus is not new canon approval. Translation awaits coauthor review.

[Source: SPOT 0.1, §6](../../../world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-6) · Source status labels: JOINTLY DEVELOPED; SASHA'S PROPOSAL; DIVERGENCE; OPEN GAP.

<a id="world_state_security"></a>

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. The central protagonist begins as a loyal foreign-intelligence or special-operations officer. The labels “CIA”, “FSB” and “GRU” in the correspondence are colloquial functional analogies. They are not the names of institutions in this world. The historical term “Okhrana” likewise does not yet determine a final organisational structure. Before an agency enters a scene, its powers, operating territory, chain of command and relationship with the army must be clear. [П5, П6]

SASHA'S PROPOSAL. A unified secret service is possible as a simplification. The protagonist's mentor belongs to its former leadership or heads special operations; a new leader shifts the focus towards internal persecution. This continuity allows employees of one apparatus to become opponents without instantly changing their biographies and beliefs. The mentor's exact position, past operations and reasons for his death are not yet fixed. [П6]

DIVERGENCE. Ivan initially envisages an independent internal-security protagonist with a future arc as ruler of the Wastelands. Sasha proposes a bureaucratically ruthless pursuer as the active representative of mechanised power. These functions may belong to different characters. Until a decision is made, no operational figure mentioned can automatically become a future head of repression or revolutionary ally.

OPEN GAP. Military recruitment, officer education, estate restrictions, reserves and the relations of regular troops with Cossacks and private factory guards are unspecified. Supply, military justice and command responsibility are not described. These questions will determine why the military protagonist can act independently, which ties he retains after going underground, and why individual units remain loyal, join the opposition or disintegrate.
