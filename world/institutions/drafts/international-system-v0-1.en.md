## Status of the world map

DIVERGENCE. International history is less settled than the empire’s internal storyline. The first batch sketches a wide world with a Russo-British imperialist war, weakened United States, fragmented Germany and Japan as the leading Asian industrial power. Later, the 1911 divergence produces a different European configuration: Russia leaves the First World War, Germany remains strong, France concludes a harsh peace and Britain seeks to regain influence. These variants cannot be mechanically combined. [П1–П4]

SASHA'S FRAME. The outside world must contribute to the causes of domestic development. A country does not create its technology, trade, armaments and intelligence operations in isolation from other powers. Market conditions and access to capital, colonies and overseas transport therefore enter canon even when an individual story remains entirely within one city. [П2, П7]

HISTORICAL BASIS. The German Empire existed from 1871, before the chosen divergence. The early formula “Germany never came together” is incompatible with unchanged history before 1911 unless an additional event is introduced. Possible subsequent fragmentation of Germany would be author-created development requiring its own cause. [ИСТ08]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. The world map should distinguish three layers: the inherited situation in 1911, alternative decisions during the first major war and the situation at the narrative’s opening. If a state in 1941 differs greatly from its prototype, a specific sequence must connect the first and third layers, rather than merely a desired visual image.

| --- | --- | --- |

<a id="world_international_britain"></a>

## Britain as a rival

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. Britain retains the role of Russia’s main external rival and a source of intelligence intrigues. In the early large-scale arc, its advantage is explained by greater organisational stability; in later sketches the British support Stolypin’s opponents, participate in the mechanised sovereign project and attempt to set continental powers against each other. None of these specific episodes amounts to an established explanation of every event in the world. [П1–П3]

DIVERGENCE. The image of a state retreating into its island after a setback needs reconciliation with a large empire, trading network and active intelligence service. It is unknown exactly what Britain loses at the end of the war, which territories and routes it retains or why it prefers indirect influence to direct confrontation. “England” in the correspondence often denotes the entire British power; the world’s official terminology needs clarification.

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. Intervention should be described through a specific interest, available instrument and limit of capability. Supporting a particular industrialist, loan or claimant produces a substantive conflict. An all-purpose puppeteer causing every Russian crisis in advance would weaken the authors’ chosen emphasis on internal contradictions and the responsibility of domestic institutions. Different British participants may also pursue incompatible goals.

<a id="world_international_europe"></a>

## Continental Europe and wars

SASHA'S PROPOSAL. In one variant, 1941 opens with Stolypin attacking a union of Germany and Austria-Hungary called the Holy Roman Empire. The name does not automatically define its borders, structure or historical continuity. Its formation date, initiator, common armed forces and neighbours’ reactions are not established. Until agreement, it should be called the proposed continental bloc. [П2]

IVAN'S PROPOSAL. Russia and Germany are in a tense peace and technological rivalry; France seeks revenge, while Britain seeks to alter the balance. Espionage, industrial sabotage and competition for specialists provide material for individual stories. These relationships may prepare a major war, but its immediate cause, participants, objectives and chronology remain open. [П3]

OPEN GAP. The outcome of the First World War, timing of Russia’s withdrawal, peace treaties and border changes are undefined. The survival of old alliances and formation of new ones are undecided. There is no answer yet to where a European conflict ends and an imperial struggle for external resources begins. A war’s scale cannot be inferred from a single movement of steam tanks; it depends on participants’ commitments, capabilities and objectives.

<a id="world_international_markets"></a>

## Markets, capital and other regions

DIVERGENCE. The formula “there is no globalism, so markets are small” is a hypothesis for the steam world’s origin in the correspondence. It requires checking baseline international trade and subsequent changes; it cannot serve as a ready-made description of 1911. Historical connections in the pre-war world through trade and capital must be considered when choosing the causes of market fragmentation. [ИСТ07]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. Restrictions on the spread of technology may be connected to competing standards, exclusive concessions, military restrictions, borrowing costs and protected markets. These mechanisms should be tied to particular states and treaties. A foreign instrument, specialist or loan then becomes the cause of a specific story rather than an arbitrary foreign threat.

EARLIER VERSION. The United States, China, Japan and the Middle East are so far described in broad expressive strokes. They have neither comparable internal structure nor clear alternative histories. A worldwide epic needs gradually to replace collective labels with actual political forces, economic interests and cultural differences. Current stories should use only what their characters truly need, leaving unknown areas open.

SASHA'S PROPOSAL. The later overarching arc envisages the victory of the new Russian association, expansion of its model and eventual disintegration. Its precise scale fluctuates between the continent and the whole world. The process of accession, resistance from other societies and role of surviving national states are not approved. The proposed arc provides a distant horizon, but does not replace an international map for every intervening era. [П1, П4]
