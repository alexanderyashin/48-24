# Energy and access

<a id="world_technology_energy"></a>

SASHA'S FRAME. Electric light and a clean affluent district should coexist with soot and hazardous everyday conditions elsewhere. Energy production for the wealthy may be located where poor people live. This is more than a difference in lamps: the comfort of some rests on labour, noise and pollution displaced onto others. Specific stations, owners and spatial distribution have not been chosen. [П4]

OPEN GAP. For each form of energy, distinguish source, conversion, transmission and end user. An electrical appliance does not imply general electrification of a district; a large generating station does not explain access to domestic lighting. Telegraphy and other electrical engineering, in turn, presuppose corresponding knowledge, production and servicing. Widespread inaccessibility of one service must not become the complete absence of an adjacent industry.

HISTORICAL BASIS. Baku’s oil industry was already substantial at the beginning of the twentieth century; regional production reached approximately eleven million tonnes in 1901. Oil and related enterprises are present in the project’s baseline reality. [ИСТ04]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. To explain the rarity of mass-market internal-combustion engines, it is more useful to investigate petroleum-product distribution, manufacturing capacity, transport costs, purchaser standards and protection of existing suppliers. Oil might be used differently, exported or controlled by a narrow coalition, but each possibility is a new authorial decision. The absence of a petrol station in a shot is insufficient to explain the entire industry.
