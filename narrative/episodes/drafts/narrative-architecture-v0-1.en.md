# Narrative architecture and the narrator

[Русский](narrative-architecture-v0-1.md) · [Wiki EN](../../../studio/navigation/en/README.md) · [SPOT 0.1 §12](../../../world/world-bible/drafts/spot-0-1-import.md#section-12)

## The larger history and the individual instalment

SASHA'S FRAME. The universe is intended to accommodate many works within shared historical boundaries. Beginning with a late event, returning to the middle, prequels, and independent stories about Cossacks, London, or supporting participants are allowed. The central arc supplies overall continuity, but the main protagonist need not appear in every sketch. An episode may have its own protagonist and its own genre emphasis. [П1; П4]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. It is useful to distinguish three scales: the historical cycle, the independent arc, and the specific scene. A cycle changes the social order; an arc resolves a significant conflict between characters; a scene presents a limited action with immediate stakes. A short video instalment may be a scene or a complete miniature, but need not retell the entire cycle. For each instalment, knowing its date, location, active forces, and irreversible consequence is sufficient. [Р1]

<a id="nar0302"></a>

## A single protagonist and an ensemble

SASHA'S FRAME. A continuing protagonist connects eras through changes in his own position: agent, opponent of the regime, creator of a new state, and its eventual guardian of order. Other principal characters express more enduring social ties. Early discussion even allows an inanimate central object, but the authors choose a person. The intelligence officer's biography later becomes the leading candidate for this function. [П1; П4–П6]

DIVERGENCE. Ivan envisages two main protagonists—a foreign intelligence officer and a domestic security officer—and proposes a path towards becoming ruler of the Wastelands for the latter. Sasha assigns the security officer the role of the intelligence officer's personal antagonist. Ivan had earlier proposed an ensemble of a worker, noblewoman, and physician. It remains unresolved whether these schemes belong to different works, different levels of one narrative, or exclude one another. The characters must not all be assigned places in a single team in advance. [П1; П2; П5; П6]

## Narrator and point of view

SASHA'S FRAME. The narrator expresses the authors' perspective, connects the narrative, and helps the reader see the meaning of events. At the same time, the central protagonist should change with his era rather than serve as the permanent mouthpiece of a single doctrine. The narrator's form is undefined: it is unknown whether the narrator exists within the world, knows the ending, addresses the audience, or states judgements directly. [П1]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. A viewpoint should be selected for each type of instalment. The intelligence officer sees assignments, reports, and the price of loyalty; the worker sees production and everyday dependence; the physician sees the patient's life and the limits of care; the noblewoman sees the rules of her own circle and encounters unfamiliar experiences. These are ways of distinguishing perspectives, not a mandatory allocation of plots. Authorial interpretation becomes more convincing when grounded in observable consequences rather than replacing them with explanatory speech. [Р1]

OPEN GAP. Narrative distance, the limits of knowledge, and the permissibility of unreliable narration are unestablished. If the same fact is presented as an official version, a memory, and an actual event, the document must distinguish them. A character's political accusation, for example of a foreign conspiracy, does not automatically become a fact of the world. This matters especially for the assassination attempt, the Romanovs' fate, and British intelligence activity. [П2–П6]

## Flashbacks and backstory

CONSENSUS. The main action begins in 1941; the period before it is treated as the past. IVAN'S PROPOSAL: show this past through the memories of Stolypin as he undergoes mechanisation, linking them to present crises and remnants of human feeling. This form creates a mystery about the Chancellor's transformation, but has not been approved as the sole means of access to the backstory. [П4]

JOINTLY DEVELOPED. The mentor offers a way to show another side of the past: carrying out secret orders and helping establish the regime. Stolypin may remember a lost ideal of service, while the operative remembers the concrete cost of that service. The reliability of their memories, discrepancies between their accounts, and how much of the events viewers can reconstruct for themselves are undefined. [П5; П6]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. Each flashback should relate to a question in the present scene: why a character trusts, what they fear, whom they recognise, or what decision they repeat. The internal chronology should nevertheless be complete enough to explain the present. Limited screen time does not relieve the authors of understanding the causes, but neither does it require all their research to appear on screen. [Р1]

<a id="nar0305"></a>

## Structure of world revelation

SASHA'S FRAME. States, enterprises, machinery brands, weapons, and production routes exist in the world whether or not an explanatory scene appears. Their origins must be traced from the historical baseline. Ivan rightly emphasises the practical need to know where resources are located and how the capital is supplied. Narrative documentation must distinguish the authors' knowledge, the characters' knowledge, and information already given to the audience. [П6; П7]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. An expressive detail can perform several tasks at once. Electric lighting shows access to infrastructure, clean clothes show the ability to avoid industrial environments, and an advertising sign shows a shared commercial language. To make a conflict intelligible, it is usually more important to show what the world's organisation means for a particular person than to list the names of institutions. The detailed arrangements remain available to the authors for later stories. [Р1]

<a id="nar0306"></a>

## Continuity and limits of perspective

OPEN GAP. The first plot, first viewpoint, way of introducing the main protagonist, and first appearance of Stolypin have not been chosen. Alternatives include an airship detective story, an investigation into engineers' murders, the physician's personal story, and street events involving the worker and noblewoman. They can be assessed as different entries into one world without declaring any one of them the beginning of canon before a separate decision. [П1–П4]

EDITORIAL PROPOSAL. An episode card supports continuity checks; its fields are given in ‘Cards for further development’. [Р1]
