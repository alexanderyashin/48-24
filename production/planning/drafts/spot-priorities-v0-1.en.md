<a id="priority_rationale"></a>

## Reasons for the sequence

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-21 block-3 -->

It is useful to distinguish the completeness of the internal world from the requirements of the first installment at the outset. This allows the backstory to be studied without an obligation to show it all immediately. The choice of audience determines methods of depiction, but does not replace decisions about social organization and the historical arc.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-21 block-4 -->

The limits of mechanization precede the final arrangement of power. If the technology is reproducible, it affects other elites, medicine, and warfare. If it is exceptional, the reason for that exceptional status and the ruler's dependence on his supporting environment must be defined. The meaning of Alexei's and Stolypin's fates depends on this.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-21 block-5 -->

The economic map rests on the calendar and technical profile. First, it is necessary to understand which industries exist and who can finance them; only then do factory districts, staffing problems, supply, smuggling, and struggles over transport routes acquire a defined meaning.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-21 block-6 -->

The social causes of the crisis connect lore to characters. Former defenders of the empire need specific changes in their position and exposure to risk before turning to opposition. The mentor's murder can be a personal turning point only after his role is decided; it does not replace an explanation of the mass movement.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-21 block-7 -->

Development of the first episode can begin before the distant ending is fully settled. An agreed slice is sufficient: location, date, operative rules, participants, and local consequences. Distant uncertainties retain their statuses if the episode does not present them as established facts.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-21 block-8 -->

<a id="priority_recording"></a>

## How to record a decision

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-21 block-9 -->

Each accepted decision receives precise wording, a status, and a basis. Related CAN, DIV, and GAP entries are then checked. Closing a question does not remove the original alternative: it remains marked as an earlier version or explicitly cancelled. A new editorial recommendation retains its own status until an authorial decision is made.

<!-- SPOT-0.1 section-21 block-10 -->

The first coherent version is ready for testing through a script when its initial state does not contradict the chronology, technical rules, and economic connections of the chosen story. This criterion concerns the sufficiency of the working model; it does not mean that every hypothesis has been definitively established as historically sound.
